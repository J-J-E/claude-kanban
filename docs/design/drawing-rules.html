Kanban Pane Drawing Rules

What a Claude Code mod can and cannot draw in the desktop app: how it is laid out, painted, ordered and layered, and how clicks, keys and drags reach it. These rules were measured on the real pane. Where the engine's type file and the real pane disagree, the real pane wins.

1Where a mod can draw

Can draw

  • The docked pane beside the chat. Opened with ui.open; it asks for a width in cells, and a width the person drags wins. This is where the board lives.
  • The band above the chat box (AbovePrompt). Up to half the window's rows; a taller tree scrolls. The "▦ Board" chip lives here and only shows while the pane is closed.
  • Toasts: short text messages, no buttons.
  • The status line: text only.

Cannot draw

  • The app's top-right menu (Files, Background tasks, Plan), its toolbars or the window frame.
  • The right-click menu. A right press over the pane always opens the app's own menu (Copy, Select All), and nothing can stop it.
  • Anything that floats over the lanes (see section 5).
  • Icons or images. Only text glyphs.

2Two drawing systems

Every pixel of the pane comes from one of two systems. They look different and take clicks differently, so every control has to pick one.

A native control can only be styled through its own few props: a Button is plain or filled, primary or secondary, dim or not. It cannot be given a custom colour (no red Delete button) or a custom width. The app draws an Input at its own fixed width with its own submit button beside it.

A Client region has a fixed width and height, cannot hold another Client, and is unmounted by any error thrown in its code. A tree may hold at most 20,000 nodes, 32 levels deep, 100,000 characters.

3Layout

  • Flexbox only. Box takes flexDirection, flexGrow, flexShrink, flexWrap, alignItems, alignSelf, justifyContent (including space-between), gap, width, height, minWidth, minHeight, margin, padding, overflow (visible or hidden), display (flex or none), and position (relative or absolute) with top, left, right, bottom.
  • Whole cells only. Every size and offset is a whole number of cells (8.25 px) or rows (20 px), or a percent. There is no half row: a card is 20 or 40 px tall, and the gap between two cards is a full 20 px row.
  • No measuring text. A width must come from the region's columns or a percent, never from counting characters, because the desktop font is proportional.
  • Dropdowns size to their value. A Box holding a Select is as wide as the chosen value unless it has a percent width, so dropdown columns only line up when each gets a percent share.
  • Truncation needs two props. A flex item does not shrink below its text. Give the Box minWidth={0} and overflow="hidden"; then a Text with wrap="truncate-end" ends in "…". Text wrap modes: wrap, truncate-start, truncate-middle, truncate-end.
  • One text size. Hierarchy comes from bold, dim, colour and space, never from size.

A bordered Box pushes its content in. The desktop insets in-flow content by at least half a row down and one cell right, whatever the padding says. So a bordered Box is only an outline and a fill; its content is one absolute child at top 0, left 0, the Box's full size, carrying the padding.
Borders are thin and round. A 1 px line with about a 10 px corner radius. A 1-row bordered Box is a pill and a 2-row Box a rounded tile. A border the same colour as the fill gives a rounded fill with no visible line, which is how cards are drawn now.
Fills poke out of round corners unless the bordered parent has overflow="hidden".
A borderless filled Box is exact, with its text centred on the row.
Colour: theme keys that follow the person's theme (text, inactive, subtle, suggestion, success, error, warning, claude, permission and more), or any hex. The board uses keys only, no hex (KB-0041): a fixed hex fill looked right in the dark theme and drew a dark bar with unreadable text in the light one, and a Client is given no theme value to choose between two palettes. In use now: card, drag ghost, menu block and Needs You header fill composerSidebarBackground (a step darker than userMessageBackground in the light theme; the first key was too faint on the real pane; fallback: userMessageBackground); hover outline inactive; open card outline and held card's outline subtle; menu row hover fill subtle; bar inactive and subtle; refused-drop text warning dim. Text on a filled Box is the default colour or a key, never a fixed colour. A test (tests/client.test.ts) fails if any drawn Box or Text carries a raw colour.
Text styles: bold, dim, italic, underline, strikethrough, inverse, background colour.
Glyphs that render cleanly: ● ○ ▶ ◆ ✕ ▾ ▸ ◂ ⋯ ▰ ▱ ▦ ✓. ▰▱ was chosen for progress: one block per plan step. ■□ drew the empty block too small.
Native controls are taller than a row (about 24 px against 20), and buttons sit about 4 px in from text above them.

5Order and layering

There is no z-index. What paints over what is decided by tree order, absolute positioning, and one hard rule about Client regions.

The app's own menus and right-click menualways on top; a mod cannot reach it
A Client region (the lanes)paints over any native Box placed over it
An absolute Boxover its earlier siblings, clipped to its pane or band
Native tree, in orderlater siblings below earlier ones on the page
Top layer first. A native popup placed over the lanes is hidden under them, which is why a card's actions are in the open card and not in a menu.
  • Tree order is page order, top to bottom: header, toolbar (Board tab only), error lines, then either the lanes, the open card or Settings.
  • An absolute Box leaves the flow and paints over earlier siblings; moving it moves nothing else. It is clipped to its pane or band.
  • Nothing can pop up over the lanes. A menu for a card would have to be drawn inside the lanes region itself, as rows under the card.
  • Scrolling belongs to the app. The pane body scrolls by wheel or keys. A mod cannot scroll it from code, cannot scroll during a drag, cannot pin a header, and has no horizontal scroll. Lanes below the visible part cannot be reached by dragging.

6Clicks, keys and drag

First click on a native control. When the pane does not hold the keyboard, the first click on a Button, Select or Input only gives it the keyboard; the press arrives with the second click. A click in the lanes acts at once. The board now asks for the keyboard on every click in the lanes, so the next button works first time (confirmed 2026-10-09). A header, toolbar or Settings control clicked straight from the chat box still needs two clicks.
Asking for the keyboard can be refused while the chat box has text, a dialog or survey is up, or the person is typing.
Right button: belongs to the app. The board ignores it.
Hover: a Box can change its border colour, background colour, display or position under the pointer. Cards use a background change. Showing hidden content on hover is unproven and not used. No tooltips, no cursor changes.
Keys: a Button can take a one-letter hotkey, which works only while the pane holds the keyboard. A Client can receive keys after a click, but Escape never arrives (it hands the keyboard back to the chat box). Keys in the lanes are unproven and unused.
Drag and drop works only in a Client. A press on a card starts a hold; moving 2 cells makes it a drag and a ghost of the card follows the pointer. The lane under the pointer gets an outline and a one-line verdict ("Drop and Claude runs it end to end."); a thin line marks the slot between two cards. A release without movement opens the card. A drop on Needs You is refused, because only Claude moves cards there. A lost release cancels the drag after 30 s.

7Timing and refresh

  • Card files are re-read once a second. Times on screen change once a minute. Nothing animates faster than a quarter second, and a moved card shows dim for up to 2 s.
  • Plugin edits reload into the running app within a few seconds to half a minute. Folds and the open card survive a reload.
  • The app stops painting the pane while another window covers it or another session is in front.
